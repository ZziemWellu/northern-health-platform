# Clinical advisor: role description

**Purpose.** A qualified clinician checks the clinical content of the platform against Ghana Health
Service protocols, and records their own decision for each module. The platform cannot be shown to a
district or a funder as clinically approved until this is done.

**Who we are looking for.** A registered midwife, a public-health physician or a community-health
nurse with recent experience in the Northern, Savannah or North East regions. Ideally someone who knows
CHPS practice and the national safe-motherhood and iCCM protocols.

**Time.** About 4 to 6 hours a month for the first three months: a review of each module, a call with
the team, and sign-off or change requests.

**What they review, one module at a time:**
1. Risk before crisis: newborn visit days, danger-sign thresholds, ranking.
2. Last-mile follow-up: referral urgency and deadlines, emergency routing.
3. Local-food nutrition: plan wording and referral direction.
4. Voice-first support: the approved reply texts in English and Twi, with a native speaker for Twi.
5. CHPS workflows: the reporting fields against the GHS registers.
6. Hidden barriers: the screening questions and their wording.
7. Sick child and prevention: the assessment steps and dose tables against the national protocol.

**What they sign.** Their own decision per module, recorded by them in `clinical_signoff.json`:
name, date and the module approved. They are never recorded as approving anything the team has not
shown them. They can also reject or request changes, and each change is logged.

**Open questions for the first meeting:**
- Which edition of the Ghana Standard Treatment Guidelines and which safe-motherhood protocol applies.
- Whether the optional day-3 newborn visit should be on by default (currently off).
- The breathing and temperature thresholds for the newborn danger-sign form.

**Terms.** [Unpaid or paid, agreed in writing, and how conflicts of interest are declared.]

Nothing in this document is an approval.
