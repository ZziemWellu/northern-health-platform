# Choosing an institutional home

Written for: the project lead. UNICEF, the World Bank, the Ministry of Health and most foundations
sign agreements with, and pay, registered organisations, not individuals. The district agreement,
the Data Protection Commission registration and the ethics application also need an organisation's
name. This note compares the routes and recommends one.

## The options

| Route | What it gives | What it costs or risks | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Your own not-for-profit** (company limited by guarantee) | Full control; the project's own name on agreements; can receive grants | Filing fee (`ngo_registration_checklist.md`), annual returns, a board, a bank account, tax filings. No track record yet, so funders may still want a partner | Weeks to register; years to build a record |
| **B. Your university** (research office or department hosts the pilot) | Credibility; ethics review through the university or GHS-ERC with institutional support; grants can run through the research office; fits the PhD | Slower contracting; overhead on grants; **check the university's intellectual-property policy first** (some claim student work); you may not control later scale-up | Depends on the research office |
| **C. A Ghanaian NGO already working in northern health** | An existing relationship with the Northern Regional and district health directorates; a track record funders recognise; field staff | You share control and credit; their priorities may differ; needs a written agreement on code ownership (it stays open source) and roles | Weeks, if one is willing |
| **D. Medic** (the organisation that builds the CHT) | Technical credibility, CHT deployment experience, contacts with ministries | A technical partner, not a local host: they do not replace A, B or C | Separate conversation |

## Recommendation

**Now: B or C to host the pilot. In parallel: A, so the project has its own home later. Talk to D early.**

- B or C gets the pilot moving: ethics, the district agreement and a small grant can go through an
  institution that already exists.
- A takes longer to become credible, so start it now and let it take over once it has a track record.
- Keep the code open source (it is AGPL already) whatever the route, so no partner can lock the
  district in.

## Questions to settle before you sign anything

1. Who owns the code and the brand? (Code: stays open source. Brand and name: say so in writing.)
2. Who is the principal investigator for ethics, and who holds the data-protection registration?
3. Where does grant money go, and what overhead does the host take?
4. What happens to the project if the partnership ends?
5. Does the university's IP policy apply to this work? Get the answer in writing.
